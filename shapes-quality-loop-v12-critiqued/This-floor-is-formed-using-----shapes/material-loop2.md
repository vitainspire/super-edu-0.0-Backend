# Identify shapes that fit without gaps
_This floor is formed using ___ shapes._

> This period focuses on the concept of tiling and gaps, building on students' understanding of shape properties from the previous lesson. It uses familiar objects like matchboxes and bangles to demonstrate how some shapes fit together perfectly while others leave gaps.

## Refresher — Door and table have straight sides
1. **Recall straight sides and corners from home objects** — Ask students what they noticed about the sides and corners of a door or table at home. Listen for descriptions of straight sides and clear corners.
2. **Trace edges to feel straight vs. curved** — Teacher traces the edge of a matchbox on the blackboard. Students trace the edge of their textbook or slate to feel a straight line.
3. **Round objects have no corners, unlike straight shapes** — Ask students what they found about round objects at home. Confirm they have no straight sides or corners, unlike doors and tables.

## Concept
1. **Floor patterns use specific shapes to fit without gaps.** — The teacher points to a tiled section of the classroom floor. Ask students to identify the shapes they see. Give them 10 seconds to look and think.
2. **Straight edges allow shapes to join perfectly, no gaps.** — The teacher draws two rectangles side-by-side on the blackboard, touching. Explain that shapes with straight edges like these can fit together perfectly, without leaving any space.
3. **Curved edges leave gaps when shapes are placed together.** — The teacher draws two circles side-by-side on the blackboard, touching at one point. Explain that shapes with curved edges will always leave gaps between them when placed together.

## Real Life
1. **Floor tiles fit with no gaps** — Teacher asks if the classroom floor has gaps. Explain that tiles fit tightly, making the floor smooth and strong. This is why we can walk on it easily.
2. **Brick walls show no gaps** — Teacher describes a brick wall. Explain that bricks, like matchboxes, have straight edges. They fit together tightly to make a strong wall without gaps.
3. **Arranging bangles leaves spaces** — Teacher asks students to imagine arranging many bangles on a table. Explain that their curved shape always leaves small spaces. This is different from how bricks or tiles fit.

## Challenge — Arrange matchboxes without gaps
1. **Arrange matchboxes to cover a slate, making a pattern** — Teacher asks students to arrange matchboxes on their slate to cover it without gaps. Students try different patterns. Teacher watches for varied arrangements, not just one solution.
2. **Discuss patterns made; what shapes fit without gaps?** — Teacher asks pairs to describe their matchbox patterns. Teacher draws one student's pattern on the blackboard. Teacher asks what shapes fit together without gaps.
3. **Arrange bangles to cover a slate, observe gaps** — Teacher asks pairs to arrange bangles on their slate to cover as much area as possible. Students observe and discuss the gaps. Teacher asks if bangles can cover without gaps.

## Level Set
1. **Floor tiles fit without gaps; bangles leave gaps.** — The teacher states that floor tiles fit without gaps because they have straight edges. Partners then discuss if bangles can cover a floor without gaps, explaining their reasoning.
2. **Reflect on easiest or most interesting part today.** — The teacher asks students to quietly think about what was easiest or most interesting in today's lesson. Students can share their thoughts with a partner.
3. **Find straight or curved edges on the way home.** — On the way home, students choose to find either one object with straight edges or one with curved edges. They observe if it sits flush against its neighbor or leaves a gap.

## Explore
*Board sketch: matchboxes arranged without gaps, bangles with gaps*

1. **Look at a wall, notice brick shapes** — Look closely at a brick wall in your village. Notice the shape of the bricks and how they fit together without leaving spaces.
2. **Find patterns on a footpath** — When walking on a footpath, look for any patterns made by stones or tiles. See if those shapes fit together perfectly or if there are gaps.
3. **Observe a bicycle wheel** — Look at a bicycle wheel. Notice its round shape. Imagine trying to cover a floor with many bicycle wheels. Think about whether they would leave gaps.

## Watch For
- **Refresher:** Students recall shape properties — Ensure students can clearly differentiate between shapes based on their properties, which is crucial for understanding why some shapes tile and others don't.
- **Concept:** Students grasp tiling vs. gaps — Observe if students understand the core idea that straight edges allow shapes to fit without gaps, while curved edges create them. Look for confusion between shape and arrangement.
- **Explore:** Students observe tiling in environment — Ensure students understand they are looking for patterns where shapes fit together or leave gaps, specifically in their surroundings.
- **Challenge:** Pairs demonstrate tiling and gaps — Check if pairs successfully arrange matchboxes without gaps and identify gaps when arranging bangles. Observe their discussions about the floor patterns in the book.
- **Level Set:** Students apply tiling understanding — Listen for students who attribute gaps to 'wrong shapes' rather than the inherent properties of the shape. Guide them to understand the geometric reason for gaps.

## Materials
- matchbox
- bangle
- slate
- paper

## Timings
Refresher: 5 min, Concept: 4 min, Reallife: 5 min, Challenge: 6 min, Level Set: 4 min, Explore: 6 min (Total: 30 min)
