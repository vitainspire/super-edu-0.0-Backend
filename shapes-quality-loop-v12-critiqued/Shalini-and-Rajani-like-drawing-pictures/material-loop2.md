# Identify objects from different perspectives
_Shalini and Rajani like drawing pictures._

> This period introduces students to the idea that objects look different from various angles, using a matchbox to demonstrate. It sets the foundation for understanding geometric shapes as representations of these views.

## Concept
1. **Observe a matchbox from above and draw it.** — The teacher draws a simple rectangle on the blackboard, explaining it's like looking straight down on a matchbox. Students observe the drawing.
2. **Observe the matchbox from the side and draw it.** — Next to the first drawing, the teacher draws a longer, thinner rectangle. Explain this is the matchbox seen from its side.
3. **Understand views change with position, not the object.** — If a child suggests the matchbox changed, the teacher explains only the view changed because our position changed. The object remains the same.

## Real Life
1. **See a house from different angles** — The teacher asks how a house looks from the road. Explain that from a nearby hill, it looks different because you see the roof and top walls.
2. **Connect views to everyday objects** — Show a cup and ask how it looks from the top. Then show its side. Explain that just like the matchbox, common objects have different views.
3. **Identify objects from their side views** — The teacher draws a side view of a cart on the blackboard. Ask students to identify the object. Explain how the side view shows the wheels and body.

## Challenge — Draw different views of a chair
1. **PLAY: Discuss chair views with a partner** — Have students turn to a partner and discuss how a chair looks from the top, front, and side. Give them one minute to talk.
2. **REFLECT: Share one view with the class** — Ask pairs to share one observation about a chair's view. Listen for descriptions of different shapes or parts seen. (Page None)
3. **ACT: Draw a chair from top, front, or side** — Instruct students to draw a chair on their slate from either the top, front, or side view. Circulate and observe their drawings. (Page None)

## Level Set
1. **Object changed or view changed?** — Ask students whether a matchbox changes when you look at its side, or if only what you see changes. Have them show thumbs up for 'object changed' or thumbs down for 'view changed'.
2. **Most interesting view to draw** — Ask students what view of an object they found most interesting to draw today. Call on a few students to share their thoughts.
3. **Notice how objects look from different places** — On the way home, look at a cart. Notice how it looks from the front and from the side. Does it look the same or different?

## Explore
*Board sketch: a matchbox from top and side*

1. **Look at a chair from above.** — At home, look at a chair from directly above. Notice its shape and what parts you can see.
2. **Observe a cup from different positions.** — Take a cup and look at it from the top, then from the side. See how the shape changes.
3. **Find an object with a distinct top view.** — Find one object in your house that looks very different from the top compared to its side. Remember what it is.

## Watch For
- **Concept:** Students understand view changes with position. — Watch for students to grasp that the object itself doesn't change, only how it appears from different angles. Clarify if they think the object transforms.
- **Explore:** Students identify object with distinct top view. — Listen for students to name a specific object and describe how its top view differs from its side view in tomorrow's refresher.
- **Challenge:** Students attempt drawing different views — Check if students are attempting to draw the chair from a specific view, even if the drawing is simple or not perfectly accurate.
- **Level Set:** Students grasp view vs. object change — Listen for students who correctly identify that the view changes, not the object, when looking from different positions.

## Materials
- matchbox
- blackboard
- chalk
- students' textbooks
- cup

## Timings
Concept: 5 min, Reallife: 4 min, Challenge: 6 min, Level Set: 5 min, Explore: 7 min (Total: 27 min)
