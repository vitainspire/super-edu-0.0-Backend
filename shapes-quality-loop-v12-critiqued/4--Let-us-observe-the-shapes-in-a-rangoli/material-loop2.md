# Identify and construct basic geometric shapes.
_4. Let us observe the shapes in a rangoli._

> This period builds on students' understanding of different views of objects by introducing them to identifying shapes within patterns and constructing them. It sets the stage for understanding how shapes fit together.

## Refresher — Recall looking at a book from above. Ask students to recall the book they looked at from directly above. Have pairs share the shape they saw. Watch for students who recall a rectangle or square. Recall looking at the book from the side. Ask students to remember the shape they saw when looking at the same book from its side. Have them discuss if it was different. Listen for 'rectangle' or 'line'. Recall the glass from directly above. Ask what shape they saw when looking at a drinking glass from directly above. Have students respond aloud. Expect 'circle' as the common answer.
1. **Recall the book viewed from above** — Ask students to recall the shape they saw when looking at a book from directly above. If confusion arises, hold up a textbook and demonstrate.
2. **Pairs share the shape they saw** — Have pairs share the shape they saw, listening for 'rectangle' or 'square'. If a student gives a wrong answer, gently guide them to re-observe the book.
3. **Recall the glass viewed from above** — Ask what shape they saw when looking at a drinking glass from directly above. Expect 'circle', and correct any misunderstandings by showing a glass.

## Concept
1. **Shapes are found in rangoli patterns.** — Draw a large, simple circle on the blackboard. Explain that this shape is called a circle. (Page None)
2. **Shapes can be described by their features.** — Draw a large square next to the circle. Point out the straight sides and corners of the square, contrasting them with the circle's curved edge. (Page None)
3. **Different shapes have different features.** — Draw a simple rangoli pattern on the blackboard, incorporating the circle and square. Point out these shapes within the pattern. (Page None)

## Real Life
1. **Rangoli uses shapes like circles and triangles.** — Explain that rangoli patterns, like those seen during festivals, use basic shapes. Point to the rangoli on the blackboard and identify a circle or triangle within it.
2. **These shapes are the same as in maths.** — Explain that the shapes in rangoli are the same geometric shapes we learn in maths class. This helps us describe what they look like.
3. **Matchsticks form different shapes with different counts.** — Show how to make a square with four matchsticks on the blackboard. Then, make a triangle with three matchsticks, explaining the stick count changes the shape.

## Challenge — Identify shapes in the rangoli
1. **PLAY: Identify shapes in the rangoli.** — Direct students to the rangoli picture in their textbook. Ask them to find circles, squares, and triangles. Give them two minutes to point them out in pairs.
2. **REFLECT: Which shapes were easy to find?** — After two minutes, have pairs quickly share with another pair which shapes they found easily. Listen for mentions of clear circles or squares.
3. **ACT: Make different shapes with match-sticks.** — Ask two students to come to the front. Give each student four matchsticks. Ask one to make a square and the other a different shape with four sticks. Ask the class to count the sticks used for each shape.

## Level Set
1. **Recap: shapes have different features.** — Briefly restate that shapes have different features, like straight sides or curved edges. Then, have partners look at the classroom door and a nearby window, and discuss which has only straight edges and which has curved edges (the door has only straight edges, the window might have a curved handle or frame).
2. **Share one new thing about shapes.** — Ask students to turn to their partner and share one new thing they learned about shapes today that they found interesting or easy to understand. Give them 30 seconds.
3. **Notice shapes on the way home.** — Invite students to notice shapes on their way home. Ask them to look for something with straight edges, like a brick, and something with curved edges, like a wheel on a cart.

## Explore
*Board sketch: simple rangoli pattern with basic shapes*

1. **Look for shapes in cooking utensils.** — Encourage students to look at cooking pots or plates at home. Notice what shapes they are. Ask them to think about why some are round and some are not.
2. **Observe shapes in clothes.** — Tell students to look at patterns or designs on their own clothes or clothes at home. See if they can find any of the shapes they learned today.
3. **Count sticks in fences or gates.** — Suggest students look at fences or gates in their village. Ask them to notice how many sticks or bars are used to make different sections or shapes.

## Watch For
- **Refresher:** Ensure students recall previous observations. — Watch for students who struggle to remember the shapes from the previous Explore. Guide them by mentioning the objects again.
- **Concept:** Check understanding of shape features. — Observe if students grasp the difference between a curved edge and straight sides/corners. Use the bangle and matchbox as clear examples.
- **Explore:** Encourage observation of shapes in daily life. — Ensure the instructions are clear for students to observe shapes in their environment, preparing them for the next lesson.
- **Challenge:** Monitor shape identification and construction. — Circulate to observe pairs identifying shapes in the rangoli. Watch the students at the front to ensure they can construct the basic shapes with matchsticks.
- **Level Set:** Gauge shape feature recognition. — Listen to partner discussions to see if students can apply the idea of straight vs. curved edges to new objects like the door and window.

## Materials

- bangle
- blackboard

## Timings
Refresher: 4 min, Concept: 6 min, Reallife: 5 min, Challenge: 8 min, Level Set: 4 min, Explore: 3 min (Total: 30 min)
