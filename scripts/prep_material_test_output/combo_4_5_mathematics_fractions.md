1. CONCEPT
----------------------------------------
  1. A fraction represents a part of a whole.
       [Show] Show a drawing on the chalkboard of a circle divided into equal parts, with some parts shaded.
  2. The denominator tells us the total number of equal parts the whole is divided into.
       [Watch for] Students often confuse the numerator and denominator, thinking the denominator represents the shaded part.
  3. The numerator tells us how many of those equal parts are being considered.
       [Deeper] This 'consideration' can mean shaded, eaten, selected, etc., depending on the context.

2. EXPERIENCE
----------------------------------------
  Divide the class into groups of four. Distribute a small handful of pebbles to each group (around 10-12). Instruct each group to divide their pebbles into two equal piles, then identify what fraction of their total pebbles is in one pile. Next, ask them to take a different number of pebbles from their total and represent that as a fraction of the original total.

3. CONTINUATION
----------------------------------------
  [x] Students in groups are actively dividing pebbles and discussing how to form piles.
  [x] Students are correctly identifying the total number of pebbles as the denominator.
  [x] Students are using Telugu to verbalize the fraction represented by their selected pebbles (e.g., 'half' or 'two-fifths').
  If not yet: If signs aren't showing, introduce a game where students draw fractions on their slates based on verbal cues and then compare with a partner.
