# Prep Material comparison

## combo_1 (emphasizes student reflection+independent exploration, minimizes hands-on activities)
- Grade 5 | Mathematics | Fractions
- Roles: Mentor, Influencer | Goals: Stay curious, Discover their interests
- Activities: Stories, Discussions, Role Play | Comfort: Improvising activities
- Resources: Whiteboard, Science Kit, Chalkboard | Language: Bilingual | Class size: <20
- Emphasize: student reflection, independent exploration | Minimize: hands-on activities

**Experience:** Begin by sharing a story about a family sharing a large chocolate bar fairly amongst themselves. As the story unfolds, use the chalkboard to visually represent the chocolate bar being divided into equal pieces for each family member. Encourage students to independently sketch their own interpretations of the chocolate bar's division on their slates, focusing on how each piece represents a portion of the whole, and then share their drawings with a partner, explaining their choices.

**Concept bullets:** Fractions represent parts of a whole. / The denominator tells us how many equal parts the whole is divided into. / The numerator tells us how many of those equal parts are being considered.

## combo_2 (emphasizes storytelling+local culture references+games, minimizes hands-on activities+independent exploration)
- Grade 5 | Mathematics | Fractions
- Roles: Guide | Goals: Think critically, Stay curious, Communicate better
- Activities: Role Play, Group Activities, Stories | Comfort: Drawing on the board, Group discussions, Moving around the classroom
- Resources: Whiteboard, Worksheets | Language: English | Class size: >40
- Emphasize: storytelling, games, local culture references | Minimize: hands-on activities, independent exploration

**Experience:** Gather students into 8 groups of 6. Tell a story about a village feast where the chief needs to share a giant flatbread equally among different families. Hand each group a large piece of paper (representing the flatbread) and ask them to fold and divide their 'flatbread' to represent different fractional portions (e.g., 'half for the elders,' 'a quarter for the children'). Use chalk to label the parts on the board as groups share their 'flatbread' divisions, narrating each step of the feast distribution.

**Concept bullets:** A fraction represents a part of a whole, where the whole is divided into equal sections. / The numerator indicates how many parts of the whole are being considered, and the denominator indicates the total number of equal parts the whole is divided into. / Fractions can be used to describe portions of objects, groups of items, or measurements.

## combo_3 (emphasizes independent exploration+critical thinking+games, minimizes hands-on activities+creativity)
- Grade 5 | Mathematics | Fractions
- Roles: Educator | Goals: Discover their interests, Apply learning to real life, Think critically
- Activities: Experiments, Demonstrations, Role Play, Stories | Comfort: Drawing on the board
- Resources: Projector, Outdoor Space, Science Kit | Language: Telugu | Class size: 20-40
- Emphasize: games, critical thinking, independent exploration | Minimize: hands-on activities, creativity

**Experience:** Divide the class into small groups. Give each group a slate and chalk. Announce a 'Fraction Challenge' where you call out a fraction (e.g., 'three-fourths'). Each group must quickly draw a shape (circle, square, or rectangle) on their slate, divide it into the correct number of equal parts, and shade in the specified number of parts. The first group to correctly display their slate wins a point.

**Concept bullets:** A fraction represents a part of a whole. / The denominator tells us how many equal parts the whole is divided into. / The numerator tells us how many of those equal parts are being considered.

## combo_4 (emphasizes independent exploration+games+creativity, minimizes storytelling+real-life connections)
- Grade 5 | Mathematics | Fractions
- Roles: Educator, Counselor | Goals: Apply learning to real life, Communicate better, Work together
- Activities: Real-life Examples, Stories, Discussions | Comfort: Group discussions, Drawing on the board
- Resources: Science Kit, Chalkboard | Language: Telugu | Class size: 20-40
- Emphasize: games, creativity, independent exploration | Minimize: storytelling, real-life connections

**Experience:** Divide the class into groups of four. Distribute a small handful of pebbles to each group (around 10-12). Instruct each group to divide their pebbles into two equal piles, then identify what fraction of their total pebbles is in one pile. Next, ask them to take a different number of pebbles from their total and represent that as a fraction of the original total.

**Concept bullets:** A fraction represents a part of a whole. / The denominator tells us the total number of equal parts the whole is divided into. / The numerator tells us how many of those equal parts are being considered.
