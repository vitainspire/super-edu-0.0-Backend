1. CONCEPT
----------------------------------------
  1. A fraction represents a part of a whole.
       [Show] Draw a circle on the board and divide it into equal parts, shading one part to represent 1/4.
  2. The denominator tells us how many equal parts the whole is divided into.
       [Deeper] The word 'denominator' comes from a Latin word meaning 'that which names or designates,' indicating the total number of parts.
  3. The numerator tells us how many of those equal parts are being considered.
       [Example] If you have a pizza cut into 8 slices (denominator) and you eat 3 slices (numerator), you've eaten 3/8 of the pizza.

2. EXPERIENCE
----------------------------------------
  Divide the class into small groups. Give each group a slate and chalk. Announce a 'Fraction Challenge' where you call out a fraction (e.g., 'three-fourths'). Each group must quickly draw a shape (circle, square, or rectangle) on their slate, divide it into the correct number of equal parts, and shade in the specified number of parts. The first group to correctly display their slate wins a point.

3. CONTINUATION
----------------------------------------
  [x] Students quickly and accurately divide their shapes into the correct number of equal parts.
  [x] Students consistently shade the correct number of parts corresponding to the numerator.
  [x] Groups collaborate effectively to represent the fraction on their slate.
  If not yet: If signs aren't showing, demonstrate several more examples on the board, explicitly narrating the division and shading process before repeating the challenge.
