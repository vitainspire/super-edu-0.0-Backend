**Refresher** *(built from Topic 2's real Explore)*
1. Recall floor tile shapes.
2. Recall brick wall shapes and how they fit together.
3. Recall what happened placing two bangles together.

**Concept**
1. Match-boxes fit without gaps — pushed together, straight edges touch perfectly.
2. Bangles leave gaps — curved edges create empty spaces.
3. Shapes with straight edges tile; curved shapes always leave gaps.

**Real Life**
1. Cricket field boundary ropes — do they fit perfectly, or are there gaps?
2. Why floor tiles (*farsh*) are usually square or rectangular — regional word used correctly.
3. Shopkeepers arrange biscuit packets (*biscuit ke dibbe*) and soap bars (*sabun*) closely; round oranges would leave gaps.

**Challenge — "This floor is formed using ___ shapes." (activity name = the book's fill-in-the-blank line, not a teacher-facing name)**
- *Play:* Each pair gets a match-box and a bangle, arranges each to cover a small area without gaps. Three minutes.
- *Reflect:* What happened with match-boxes vs. bangles — gaps or not.
- *Act:* Identify floor shapes on Page None — first floor uses squares, second uses rectangles.

**Level Set**
1. Recap gaps/no-gaps; partners discuss whether all shapes tile.
2. Share what you learned about gaps.
3. Find one thing that fits tightly (bricks) and one that leaves gaps (round stones) on the way home.

**Explore**
1. Tell students to look at their floor's tile/brick shapes and how they fit.
2. Tell students to observe a stack of rotis for gaps.
3. Tell students to observe a stack of books for gaps.

Materials used: textbook, match-box, bangle.
