**Concept**
1. Object views depend on looking position. *Hold up a small object like a matchbox. Ask students to describe what they see. Walk around the object slowly, showing different perspectives to the class. (Page 11)*
2. Top, front, and side views are distinct. *Show the matchbox from the front, then the side, then directly from above. Draw simple sketches of each view on the blackboard as seen, labelling them. (Page 12)*
3. Same object, different shapes from views. *Point to the sketches on the board. Explain that the object itself does not change, but what is seen depends on where one looks from. (Page 12)*

**Real Life**
1. House views from different angles. *Ask students what a house looks like from the front, then from the side. Point to the house drawings on page 12. (Page 12)*
2. Identifying house views in the book. *Explain that the book shows three views of a house. Ask students to identify which drawing is the top view, front view, and side view. (Page 12)*
3. Common objects have different views. *Show a cup. Ask students to imagine its top view (a circle) and its side view (a rectangle). Point out the pictures of everyday objects in the book. (Page 12)*

**Challenge — Role Play**
- *Play:* Pairs act out looking at a chair. Divide students into pairs. Assign one student to be a "chair" and the other to "look" from different positions. Give 30 seconds for each position.
- *Reflect:* Ask pairs to discuss for 30 seconds what was easiest about describing the chair from different angles. Listen for observations about distinct features.
- *Act:* Label chair views in the book. Direct students to the chair drawings on page 11. Ask them to identify which drawing is the top view and which is the side view. The drawing that looks like a plain rectangle is the top view.

**Level Set**
1. Recap views, check understanding. Restate that objects look different from the top, front, and side. Ask partners to decide if an object changes its actual shape or only how it appears from different views.
2. Easiest or most interesting today. Ask students to share with a partner what they found easiest or most interesting about today's lesson.
3. Find objects with changing views. On the way home, look for a bicycle or a bullock cart. Notice how it looks different when seen from the front, side, and directly from above. Nothing to bring back.

**Explore**
1. Tell students that at home, they should look at a chair from directly above. Ask them to notice its shape and what parts they can see.
2. Tell students that they should look at their own house or a neighbour's house from the front and from the side. Ask them to notice the differences.
3. Tell students to pick up a small object like a cup or a book and look at it from the top, front, and side. Ask them to notice how it appears from each angle.
