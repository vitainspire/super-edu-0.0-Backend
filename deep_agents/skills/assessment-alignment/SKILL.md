# Assessment alignment

The textbook is the syllabus and the printed questions are what the child is
examined on (`core-pedagogy`). Home study cannot be assumed: no second book,
no printables, no guaranteed quiet space or light, no guaranteed adult who
reads the medium of instruction. **So everything a pass requires happens inside
the period, or it does not happen.**

This is not teaching to the test. It is refusing to build a plan whose success
depends on resources this child does not have.

## What a pass actually requires

Three things, and only the first is usually planned for:

1. The child understands the idea. Handled by the trajectory
   (`experience-design`).
2. The child recognises the **question form** the book uses for it. "Which of
   these is a triangle?", "Write the number", "Match the following", "Fill in
   the blanks", "Draw the top view". A child who understands the idea and has
   never seen it asked this way loses the marks anyway.
3. The child has **something to revise from**. Almost always their own
   notebook, because it is the only page in their house.

Plan for all three. Two out of three is the commonest reason a well-taught
class fails a paper.

## Contact with the printed question, in class

Every period should put **at least one of the chapter's own printed questions**
in front of the class, in the book's own wording, and have the class answer it
out loud or with hands. Not as the lesson - as 40 seconds inside Concept or at
the end of Challenge.

Order matters and it does not bend:

    RIGHT   handle the object -> say the idea -> read the page -> now answer
            question 3 on page 12 the way the book asks it
    WRONG   read question 3, teach the answer, then handle the object if
            minutes remain

The second order produces a child who can complete this exercise and cannot
answer the same idea phrased any other way - which is what "learned it for the
test" means (`core-pedagogy`).

## The exercise ledger

Across a chapter's forty periods, track **which printed questions have been
met in class** the same way `lesson-design` tracks vocabulary:

    per period   the page and question numbers this period puts in front of
                 the class
    per chapter  the printed questions no period has covered

An uncovered exercise at the end of the chapter is a real finding, and it is
one nobody else in the pipeline can see. Report it rather than quietly leaving
it: the teacher can then spend a review period on exactly those items.

Use `get_page_range` and `search_book` to find the exercise pages - they are
part of the chapter and are frequently the thinnest-planned pages in it
(`grounding`). Never cite a question number you have not read.

## The one notebook line

**Every period leaves exactly one revisable thing in the notebook**, and it is
written by the child, in about 60-90 seconds at the end of Level Set.

Its shape:

    the child's own sentence   in the words they used in class
    + the book's term          the word the exam will use
    + one mark of the thing    a tiny drawing, or the numeral, or the shape

Example:

    "Looking straight down = top view."  and a 2 cm sketch of the bag from
    above.

Rules that keep it from becoming a copying exercise:

- **One line, not a paragraph.** Forty children copying five lines is the rest
  of the period.
- **Not dictated verbatim** where the child can produce it: ask the class to
  say it, take the clearest version, then write that on the board once.
- **The teacher does not collect it.** A closure with forty notebooks to mark
  will not happen. Check it by walking one row, or by having the row read
  theirs to each other.
- **It matches the exam's term.** The child's own words are the route in; the
  printed word has to be on the page they revise from, or revision teaches them
  a term nobody will ask about.

**One notebook moment per period, not two.** The take-home question
(`self-study`) also wants a line, and forty children opening notebooks twice
costs more minutes than either line is worth. Write both in the same 90 seconds,
at the end of Level Set: the closure line, then the take-home question under it
in a handful of words. The board carries both while the class copies once.

## What this does not do

Say the limit honestly rather than implying a guarantee. Exam contact, a
notebook line and a named floor raise a child's chances substantially; they do
not promise a pass. A child absent for the period, a child who is two years
behind in reading rather than in this topic, and a paper that asks something
the chapter never printed are all still real. What this file rules out is the
one failure the pipeline is responsible for: a plan that was only ever going to
work for children who could study at home.

## Exams ask across topics; periods teach one

A paper mixes topics from the whole chapter, and a child taught in forty
airtight periods meets that mixture for the first time in the exam hall. Cheap
countermeasures, no extra minutes:

- The Refresher already reaches backwards one period (the seam). Roughly once a
  strand, reach back **two or three** instead, using the same seam wording.
- Where a topic is a review or consolidation topic, use it for exactly this:
  the printed questions from several earlier topics, answered in class.

## No marking load, ever

Anything requiring the teacher to mark thirty-plus pieces of work will not
survive the week. Prefer checks that are instant and visible: choral answer,
thumbs, everybody holds up their slate at once, one row reads to the next
(`engagement`). This is what makes daily assessment possible at all.

## What this never justifies

- Drilling question forms before the child has handled the thing.
- Narrowing what is taught to what is examinable. The chapter's content is
  fixed; the exam contact is added to it, not carved out of it.
- Reducing the target because the exam only asks the easy version
  (`contextual-reinforcement`).

## If the pipeline has no field for this

The exercise ledger and the notebook line are content, not schema. Where no
field exists yet, write them into the sections that already exist - the printed
question inside Concept, the notebook line at the end of Level Set - and note
in your output that a dedicated field would let the coverage check run
automatically.
