# Engagement

Engagement is not enjoyment and it is not novelty. It is **the fraction of the
room doing something observable right now**. A lively-sounding period in which
the teacher talks and one bright child answers has an engagement rate of about
one child in thirty, and it reads perfectly well on paper.

## The participation floor

**No stretch longer than about 90 seconds in which only the teacher acts.**

That is the whole rule, and it survives contact with the room because the
devices that break the stretch are free: a question everybody answers with
their fingers costs no materials, no movement, and 15 seconds.

Ask of every section: *at this moment, what are all thirty children doing?* If
the honest answer is "listening", the clock is running down on their attention
and the next thing that happens should be something they do.

## Private response, or the quiet child never answers

A child two years behind the syllabus will not raise a hand in front of forty
peers, and a hands-up count tells the teacher only about the confident. Every
period needs at least one device where **the teacher gets a count and no child
is exposed**:

- **Thumbs on the chest.** Up for yes, sideways for not sure. Held against the
  body so neighbours cannot see; the teacher, standing at the front, can.
- **Eyes down, hands up.** Class looks at the desk, hands go up, teacher
  counts, hands down.
- **Show me with your fingers.** One finger for the first option, two for the
  second. Everyone answers at once, so nobody is singled out.
- **Everybody says it together.** Choral answer hides the child who is half a
  beat behind and still gets them saying the word.
- **Tell your neighbour, not the class.** Ten seconds, no movement, no
  reporting back. Every child in the room speaks the idea once.

The Level Set section depends on this: a wrong belief is only admitted
honestly when admitting it is not public (`misconception-analysis`).

## The attention arc across 22 minutes

Attention is not flat, and the six sections of `lesson-design` are not equally
at risk:

    Refresher   2 min    high attention, wasted if spent revising. Open with
                         a question, not a summary
    Real Life   4 min    the class handles or stands over the thing. Highest
                         participation in the period - use it
    Concept     5 min    THE DANGER ZONE. The page is open, the teacher is
                         reading, thirty children are watching
    Challenge   7 min    hands busy; risk moves from attention to management
    Level Set   3 min    needs a private-response device to work at all
    Explore     1 min    a question they leave with, not a summary

**The Concept fix.** Never read the page at a class that is holding nothing.
Break the five minutes with two 20-second checks, and keep the object from Real
Life in their hands or on the desk while the page is read, so the printed
drawing is compared to a thing rather than received. "Look at the picture on
page 4. Now look at your bag from the top. Same or different? Thumbs."

## Movement is free; furniture is not

Standing, turning, pointing, hopping, holding up fingers, walking round one
object, everybody looking up at the ceiling - all free and all instant. Forming
groups costs 5-8 minutes (`activity-design`). So the default engagement device
is movement **from where the child is sitting**, and group re-forming happens
at most once in a period, in Challenge, if the activity truly needs it.

Whole-class staging that needs nothing but chalk and a floor is listed per
subject in `early-math` and `literacy`. Prefer those to anything requiring
material distribution.

## Signals that the section died, and what the teacher does

Write these into the plan where they matter - a teacher reading a sheet mid-
period cannot diagnose from first principles:

    silence from most of the room      the question was too abstract. Point at
    when a question is asked           the object and ask about that instead

    the same three children answering  switch to a device where everybody
                                       answers at once

    children looking at each other     the instruction was unclear. Do one
    instead of the object              yourself, out loud, then re-ask

    finished in half the time          the stretch prompt exists for exactly
                                       this minute (`stretch-design`)

## Novelty is not engagement

A format used four periods running becomes a routine the class performs without
thinking; a different format every period costs the class the time it takes to
learn each one, and that comes out of the 22 minutes. The pipeline's no-repeat
window (`activity-design`) is the balance point: vary the format across a
strand, keep the *devices* above constant so they cost no explanation.

## Roles, only if they carry the concept

Handing a child a job keeps them occupied; it only keeps them learning if the
job is part of the idea. Holding a digit card in a human number machine is the
concept. Distributing chalk is not. Where you name a role, name what the child
holding it is thinking about.
