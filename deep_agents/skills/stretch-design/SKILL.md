# Stretch design

The child who finishes the activity in a third of the time is the child this
pipeline teaches least. They spend ten minutes of every period waiting, learn
that school is easy, and arrive at the year where it stops being easy with no
practice at being stuck.

Stretch is not extra work. It is **the same content at higher demand**, offered
to whoever wants it, costing the teacher nothing.

## Invited demand versus required demand

`core-pedagogy` bounds demand by the grade band - and it is right, because
over-pitching a class is the commonest invisible failure. The bound applies to
what the class is **required** to do and what the assessment asks. It does not
apply to an invitation:

    required   every child, graded, inside the grade band's verb list
    invited    whoever wants it, never graded, may sit a band above -
               because failing an invitation costs the child nothing

So a Grade 3 class is not asked to *justify a generalisation* about place
value. A Grade 3 child who has finished may absolutely be asked "can you make
one where that trick doesn't work?" and be left to chew on it. The difference
is who is on the hook.

Call `lookup_grade_constraints` for the required verbs. For the invited prompt,
one band up is the useful reach; two bands up is a prompt nobody can enter.

## The six moves

Take the task the class just did and apply one. All six cost zero materials and
zero marking:

1. **Invert it.** Give the answer, ask for the question. "This is the top view
   - what could the object be? Find two different answers."
2. **Break it.** Ask for a case where the rule fails or the pattern stops.
   "Find a shape where the top and side views look the same."
3. **Constrain it.** Same task, one thing forbidden. "Make it again, without
   using a square."
4. **Minimise or maximise.** "What is the smallest number of matchsticks that
   still makes three triangles?"
5. **Explain it to someone who disagrees.** "Ravi says a triangle standing on
   its point isn't a triangle. What would you show him?" (This is a stretch;
   handing them a struggling neighbour to teach is a job.)
6. **Generalise it.** "Does that work for every box, or only this one? How
   would you find out?"

Move 2 and move 6 are the ones that produce actual thinking, and both are one
sentence long.

## What makes a stretch prompt run in this room

    same materials     whatever the class already has in hand. A prompt needing
                       a new object is a prompt that does not happen
    one sentence       the teacher says it once, to one corner of the room
    more than one
    right answer       so it cannot be "finished" and does not need marking
    visible ending     the child shows a neighbour or the row. Not collected
    same concept       deeper into this idea, never ahead into next chapter's
                       content - running ahead in the book makes the next
                       period's teaching harder, not easier

And the test that catches most bad ones: **would a child who finished early
rather do this than nothing?** A second worksheet fails that test honestly.

## Written in the plan, not left to the teacher

A plan that says "provide extension for advanced learners" has handed the
teacher the hard part. Write the actual sentence, at the point in the period
where the first child finishes - usually 3-4 minutes into Challenge:

    Challenge (7 min)
      ...
      For whoever finishes first: "make a shape with exactly ten matchsticks
      that is not a square or a rectangle - then find someone whose shape is
      different from yours and count both sets of sides."

One sentence. That is the whole deliverable.

## Creative thinking is a prompt shape, not a topic

Creativity here is not an art activity bolted on. It is what happens when a
question has more than one right answer and the child has to choose. Two
sentence shapes carry almost all of it:

    "find another way..."
    "make one that..."

Both are free, both are open, and both work in maths, language and EVS
without adaptation.

## Do not let the stretch replace the floor

The capable child's prompt is worth one sentence of the plan. It must never
take minutes away from the section where the class as a whole is meeting the
conceptual jump, and it must never become the thing the teacher spends the last
five minutes on while a third of the room has not reached the floor
(`differentiation`).

If you have written a rich stretch and no floor, you have written a period for
the top of the class.

**The teacher's attention is the scarce resource, not the minutes.** During
Challenge it belongs to the children who have not reached the floor. So a
stretch prompt must be answerable without the teacher: no judging, no checking,
no explaining it a second time. The child shows a neighbour and the teacher
hears about it later or not at all. A prompt that pulls the teacher across the
room has cost the floor children their only minutes of one-to-one help.

## The boilerplate test

A stretch prompt that would work verbatim for any topic in any chapter is not a
stretch prompt. It has to name **this period's object, number or word**:

    boilerplate  "advanced learners can explore the concept further"
    boilerplate  "give an extra challenge to fast finishers"
    real         "add one line to that figure so it has exactly two more
                 triangles"

If you cannot write the real version, say the topic has no cheap stretch rather
than shipping the boilerplate - a teacher reading the second version learns
nothing they did not already know.
